# UI review and selected-area information hierarchy

## Scope

Review prompted by the desktop toolbar and Markham tract 5350403.16 screenshots. Changes are limited to frontend layout, information presentation, and regression tests. No source datasets, backend endpoints, hosting plans, or production deployments were changed.

## Findings and fixes

- Toolbar controls previously mixed 40px, 44px and 46px outer heights. The header also competed with the title for horizontal space. Controls now occupy a dedicated responsive grid, use consistent 44px outer heights, and have aligned field captions. Tablet/mobile layouts use deliberate rows instead of incidental wrapping.
- The Housing snapshot and Selected Geography panel repeated the same local Census values. The snapshot is now regional context only: selecting an area replaces it with one local profile; clearing the selection restores it.
- Every selected area previously expanded Census, rental, construction and transit statistics regardless of the chosen metric. The relevant topic now appears first and opens automatically. Other topics are accessible through native keyboard-operable disclosures. Changing the metric or area resets the topic presentation. Actual DOM order matches visual order.
- The selected metric has a restrained accent background and border. Local metric cards use a consistent two-column grid, equal-height rows, tabular numbers and reserved source-label space. This avoids three-column labels wrapping unpredictably in the 430px sidebar.
- Construction options now have their own dropdown group. Transit shows “GTFS snapshot,” not a misleading disabled Census year.
- Missing selected rental figures explicitly say “Not published” or “Not available,” rather than disappearing or appearing as zero. A missing housing component no longer becomes zero when calculating combined dwelling totals or tenure shares.
- CMHC construction estimates use CMHC-specific provenance, not a tooltip claiming a suppressed Statistics Canada figure. Existing official/derived/estimated flags, survey-zone and municipal fallback labels, reference years, transit coverage limitations and full CSV export remain available.
- The dark-mode derived-value label was lightened after accessibility testing measured insufficient contrast against the new selected-card background.

## What each metric choice should show first

| Dropdown choice | First topic | Supporting information |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rent burden, affordability index, median income, median rent | Household & housing profile | Four related Census measures, with the chosen one highlighted once |
| Population, population growth | Population & growth | Population and 2016–2021 change; small-base warnings retained |
| Vacancy rate, average rent (CMHC) | Rental market | Published rates/rents, unit-size rents and rental universe; source geography and survey year disclosed |
| Housing starts, completions | Housing construction | Starts, completions, under-construction and unabsorbed counts; calendar year and estimate provenance |
| Transit access score, transit routes nearby | Transit accessibility | Score and route count, method, snapshot date, included/missing agencies |

Rent burden and rent-to-income are not interchangeable: one measures the share of renter households above a shelter-cost threshold; the other compares area medians. Affordability index is derived from the same income/rent inputs, so a second ratio tile was not carried into the consolidated local profile. Census median rent and CMHC average rent also cover different samples and reference periods; they remain in distinct topics rather than being treated as duplicate values.

## Review dimensions

- **Copy:** reduced repeated instructions; distinguish Census, CMHC construction, rental surveys and transit snapshots.
- **Visual hierarchy:** one selected-area profile; active topic first; supporting information progressively disclosed.
- **Colour:** reuse the existing civic palette and light/dark accent tokens; retain text-based provenance rather than relying on colour alone.
- **Typography:** consistent labels and numeric sizing; keep definitions available through existing information buttons.
- **Spacing:** shared toolbar baselines and uniform local card grids; responsive bounds checked rather than inferred from one desktop screenshot.
- **Experience:** preserve search, URL/history state, clearing selection, comparison pins, CSV export, keyboard interactions and source disclosure.

## Verification

- Production build, TypeScript check, lint and 62 unit tests passed.
- Targeted browser tests exercise every supported metric at municipality/tract level, active topic order, non-duplication, disclosure keyboard access, unavailable values, and suppression-safe housing calculations.
- Toolbar measurements cover 1280, 1365, 1600 and 1920px. Selected-profile overflow and card-height checks cover 320, 375, 390, 768, 1024, 1280 and 1440px.
- Automated accessibility checks cover the selected profile, dark mobile presentation, and the existing overview/transit journeys.
- The first wider run passed 76/77 checks. Its remaining failure was a test attempting to click through the open search dropdown onto the map heading. The test now clicks an uncovered heading and retains its outside-dismissal/Escape assertions.
- Production-mode regression run: 73/78 passed initially. The five affected checks then passed in the standard local test configuration: one obsolete duplicate-badge assertion was corrected; dark-mode derived-label contrast was fixed; three WebKit journeys had been blocked by the production HTTPS-upgrade policy on an HTTP-only loopback server. The production security policy was not weakened. Trace evidence showed HTTPS script-load failures rather than a UI hydration bug.
- All 78 regression checks have passing results across the full run and affected-test rerun, including Chromium journeys and Firefox/WebKit smoke coverage. This is not a claim that all three engines were tested against an HTTPS production deployment.
- The six focused UI checks were rerun after the final fixes: 6/6 passed. Final lint, 62/62 unit tests, and the production build (including TypeScript) also passed. Desktop/light-mobile and dark selected-profile screenshots were visually reviewed.

Automated checks and visual inspection do not replace usability sessions with people unfamiliar with the data. A useful next observation is whether users can correctly distinguish Census rent burden, the derived affordability index, and CMHC rental-market figures without assistance.
